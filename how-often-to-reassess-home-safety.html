How Often Should You Re-Check Your Home's Safety?

Guide · AgingInPlaceChecklist

A home safety checklist isn't something to take once and file away. The layout of the house doesn't change, but the person living in it does — and it's usually a change in the person, not the house, that turns a low-risk home into a moderate or higher-risk one. Here's when it's actually worth retaking the checklist, rather than assuming last year's result still holds.

Events that should trigger a recheck

  • A fall, even a minor one. Our checklist weighs fall history heavily for a reason — one fall meaningfully raises the odds of another. It's worth reassessing the home immediately, not at the next annual checkup.
  • A new cane, walker, or wheelchair. The moment a mobility aid enters daily life, doorway widths, rugs, and entry steps stop being minor annoyances and start being real barriers.
  • A new diagnosis or medication change. Conditions affecting balance, vision, or energy — and some medications — change fall risk even when nothing in the house has moved.
  • A hospital stay or surgery, even unrelated to mobility. Coming home after a hospitalization, especially for older adults, often means temporarily reduced strength and balance while the home hasn't changed to match.
  • An aging parent or relative moving in. A home that's safe for the people who currently live there may not be safe for a new resident with different needs — this is worth a fresh look, not an assumption.
  • Any renovation, even a small one. New flooring, a remodeled bathroom, or moved furniture can change lighting, walking paths, and hazards in ways that are easy to miss because they seem like improvements.

Even without a clear trigger

Some risk factors — nighttime lighting, rug placement, general clutter along walking paths — drift gradually and don't announce themselves the way a fall or a new diagnosis does. Revisiting the checklist roughly once a year, even when nothing obvious has changed, catches the slow drift that a single "we checked this already" doesn't.

It only takes two minutes

The reason a yearly recheck is realistic at all is that the checklist is short by design — 12 questions, most homes skip a few based on earlier answers, done in under two minutes. That's specifically so it's something you'd actually do again, not a project that requires setting aside an afternoon.

It's been a while, or something's changed? Retake the checklist — it takes under two minutes either way.
