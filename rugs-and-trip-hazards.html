The Most Overlooked Fall Hazard: Loose Rugs and Runners

Guide · AgingInPlaceChecklist

Of everything on our home safety checklist, loose rugs are the item people are most likely to shrug off — it's just a rug. But the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention consistently identifies falls as the leading cause of injury among older adults, and loose flooring is one of the most commonly cited environmental contributors. Unlike doorway width or entry steps, it's also one of the very few hazards on the checklist you can fully fix this weekend for under $20.

Why a rug is more dangerous than it looks

A rug doesn't need to be worn out or torn to cause a fall. The problem is almost always one of three things: an edge that curls or lifts enough to catch a foot, a walker leg, or a wheelchair caster; a rug that isn't secured and slides on a hard floor the moment weight shifts onto it; or a rug placed at a transition — a doorway, the top of a stairway, the edge of a hallway — where someone's attention is already on the step ahead, not the floor underfoot.

Where it matters most

  • Hallways and walking paths. Anywhere someone walks regularly, especially at night with lower visibility.
  • Room-to-room transitions. The edge where a rug meets hard flooring is where trips are most likely.
  • Stairs. Runners on stairs combine a trip hazard with a fall that has real consequences. This is the one location where removing the rug entirely is usually the right call.
  • Entryways. Often wet or snowy shoes make an already-loose mat worse.

How to fix it without giving up the rug

You don't have to choose between style and safety in every room. A few reliable fixes:

  • Double-sided rug tape or a non-slip rug pad underneath, sized to the full rug — not just the corners — so the whole surface stays flat and anchored.
  • Low-pile over high-pile where possible; thick or shaggy rugs are more likely to catch a foot, cane tip, or walker leg.
  • Tapered or beveled edges on any rug that sits at a room transition, so there's no lip to catch.
  • Full removal from stairs and from any hallway that's a primary walking path, especially at night.

None of this requires a contractor or a renovation — it's the kind of fix that can happen the same day it's noticed, which is exactly why it's worth checking now rather than after something happens.
